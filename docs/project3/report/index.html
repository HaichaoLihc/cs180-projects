Part A · 1.1–1.3

Noise and reconstruction

PixNerd operates on 512 × 512 RGB images in [−1, 1]. I reuse one puppy image and one noise tensor at t = 0.25, 0.50, 0.75. Time runs from noise (0) to image (1).

xt = (1 − t)ε + tx1   ·   ε ∼ N(0, I)

Gaussian blur and one-step reconstruction

Blur first restores the amplitude by dividing by t. The learned method predicts the remaining update using “a high quality photo.” All states and model evaluations use float32.

x̂blur = Gσ(xt / t)   ·   x̂one-step = xt + (1 − t)vθ(xt, t, c)

Baseline choice. Selected σ by lowest source MSE: t = 0.25: σ = 16.0, MSE 0.01519; t = 0.50: σ = 8.0, MSE 0.00543; t = 0.75: σ = 4.0, MSE 0.00157. Blur suppresses noise but loses fur detail; the learned estimate restores the eyes and nose.

Part A · 1.4–1.5

Velocity diagnostics and integration

The true velocity is vgt = x1 − ε. Predicted and true updates share a symmetric RGB display scale; zero is middle gray. Error is computed before clipping, with one heatmap scale across times.

Et = √ meanRGB [((1 − t)(v̂t − vgt))²]

Euler reconstruction · 1.5

Starting at t = 0.50, take 25 updates with h = 0.02, recomputing velocity each time. Shorten the final step when necessary to end at exactly t = 1.

xt+h = xt + h vθ(xt, t, c)
Evidence & interpretation

At t = 0.50, MSE is 0.005430 for blur, 0.001504 for one step, and 0.001898 for Euler. Euler adds sharper fur, but one step is closer under pixel MSE.

Part A · 1.6, 2.1, 3.3–3.4

Generation and visual illusions

Five noise seeds (180–184), Euler h = 0.02, and “a high quality photo.” Guidance reuses the same noise for a paired comparison.

vCFG = vc + 7(vc − vu)
Evidence & interpretation

The generic prompt produces portraits in all five cases. Guidance repairs incomplete faces and distorted anatomy, but changes identity and composition; it improves consistency without increasing subject diversity. Some skin remains overly smooth.

Anagrams and hybrids · 3.3–3.4

Anagrams average guided velocities in upright and 180°-rotated coordinates. Hybrids combine low frequencies from one prompt with high frequencies from another (Gaussian σ = 16, kernel 265).

vanagram = ½[v₁(x) + R⁻¹v₂(Rx)]   ·   vhybrid = G(v₁) + v₂ − G(v₂)
Evidence & interpretation

Both anagrams support their intended readings. The cat/forest scale change is convincing but imperfect, with a cyan cast despite the black-and-white prompts. The skull/waterfall hybrid is a partial failure: the reduced image suggests a head, without strong skull features.

Part A · 3.1

Image-to-image edits

Start at (1 − t)ε + tx1, then integrate with h = 0.02 and guidance 7. Each panel shows the source followed by t = 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.10, 0.14, 0.25, 0.50. Prompt: “a high quality photo.”

Photographs. More source signal restores the Campanile clock and the lake’s hills and water. Low t can replace the subject entirely: the puppy becomes human portraits, and the lake becomes a car. The puppy is recognizable at t = 0.50, but remains soft.

Painting. Van Gogh’s field becomes a photographic-looking landscape at t = 0.25; t = 0.50 retains more brushwork.

Hand drawings. Low t often gives frames, pale surfaces, or unreadable marks. At t = 0.50 the sailboat and teapot outlines survive while the screenshot grid fades. Neither sweep produces a convincing photograph of the intended object. The supplied screenshots were square-padded before resizing, preserving all original strokes.

Part A · 3.2

Text-guided edits

The same seven starting times and sampling settings now use subject-specific prompts. Lower t allows stronger changes; higher t preserves more of the original scene.

Prompt: “a photograph of a lighthouse on a rocky coast under a clear blue sky”

Low t gives a coastal lighthouse; intermediate t mixes lighthouse and Campanile details. At t = 0.50 the source tower dominates.

Prompt: “a photograph of a golden retriever puppy wearing a red knitted scarf sitting in snow”

The specific prompt resolves the generic-prompt portrait failure. Low t produces a new puppy in snow; higher t restores the source face and background while weakening the scarf and snow.

Prompt: “a photograph of a snowy alpine lake at dawn, mountains reflected in the water”

Low t rebuilds the scene as an alpine valley. Intermediate t restores the original horizontal composition; t = 0.50 preserves the source hills and clouds. The warm sunset palette remains compatible with dawn.

Part B · 1.1–1.2.1

MNIST setup and denoising

All five independent models train on the full 60,000-image MNIST training split with Adam. A UNet uses two downsampling stages, a 7 × 7 → 1 × 1 bottleneck, transpose-convolution upsampling, and concatenated skip connections; blocks use batch normalization and GELU. Test images are held out.

Part B · 1.2.2–1.2.3

Noise robustness and the mean-image limit

Unseen noise levels · 1.2.2

Keep digit identity and noise realization fixed while varying σ.

Evidence & interpretation

Mostly readable through σ = 0.6. At 0.8–1.0, extra and fragmented strokes appear. Even clean inputs change slightly (MSE 0.003359).

Regressing from pure noise · 1.2.3

Input ε and target digit x are independent. Under squared error, the optimal output is the training-image mean:

D*(ε) = 𝔼[x | ε] = 𝔼[x]
Evidence & interpretation

Outputs converge toward nearly identical blurry, 0/8-like averages. At epoch 5, variance across inputs is 0.0000459 and MSE to the training centroid is 0.0000707. The model learns shared strokes, not distinct samples.

Part B · 2.1–2.6

Learning and sampling a flow

Train on xt = (1 − t)x0 + tx1, with x0 ∼ N(0, I) and t ∼ Uniform[0, 1], to predict x1 − x0. Two fully connected blocks embed t and modulate the bottleneck and first upsampling stage. The exponential schedule multiplies LR by 0.11/10 after each epoch.

L = 𝔼 ‖uθ(xt, t) − (x1 − x0)‖²

Time-conditioned sampling · 2.1–2.3

Start from Gaussian noise and take 50 Euler steps. The same 40 initial noises are used across checkpoints.

Evidence & interpretation

Fragmented epoch-1 outputs become varied, readable digits by epoch 5. Epoch 10 adds modest improvement, with some ambiguous shapes remaining; mean training MSE falls from 0.18320 to 0.09321.

Class conditioning · 2.4–2.6

Embed one-hot labels and modulate features as cemb ⊙ features + temb. Drop the class vector with probability 0.1. Sampling uses 300 Euler steps and uu + 5(uc − uu).

Evidence & interpretation

By epochs 5–10, most requested classes are clear, with varied stroke shapes. These grids are more legible than time-only samples, but sampling uses more steps (300 vs. 50), so this is not a controlled test of conditioning alone.

Part B · 2.6 extension

Remove the scheduler

Train a fresh class-conditioned model at a constant LR of 0.001, reduced from the scheduled model’s initial 0.01. The smaller fixed update compensates for removing late-training decay. Architecture, initial seed, data order, training noise, label dropout, guidance, and sampling steps are matched.
