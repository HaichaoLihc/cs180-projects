A.2: Recover Homographies

For each pair, 16 spatially distributed correspondences are stored in JSON. SIFT supplies candidate locations; a small custom RANSAC loop rejects outliers. The displayed pairs are the final inputs to computeH. This assistance is separate from the required solver: computeH constructs the 2N by 8 system and uses numpy.linalg.lstsq. Coordinates are centered and scaled to mean distance sqrt(2) before solving; H is then denormalized and divided by H[2,2]. No built-in homography solver is used.

u = (h11*x + h12*y + h13) / (h31*x + h32*y + 1)
v = (h21*x + h22*y + h23) / (h31*x + h32*y + 1)

[x y 1  0 0 0  -u*x -u*y] h = u
[0 0 0  x y 1  -v*x -v*y] h = v

h = [h11 h12 h13 h21 h22 h23 h31 h32]^T
minimize ||A h - b||^2;  H = inv(T_dst) H_norm T_src

With only four nondegenerate pairs, eight equations can determine H exactly. Extra points reduce sensitivity to click or feature-localization noise. Collinear or coincident configurations are rejected. The errors below are training residuals on the selected matches, not independent estimates of full-image accuracy.

A.3: Warp and Rectify Images

H maps source coordinates into the reference plane. Transform the four source corners to determine inclusive canvas bounds, retain the world-coordinate origin, and apply inverse H to every destination pixel. Divide by the homogeneous coordinate before sampling. Out-of-bounds samples receive a false validity mask, while legitimate black pixels stay valid. A projective horizon crossing or excessive canvas size raises an error.

Nearest neighbor rounds each inverse-mapped coordinate to one source pixel. Bilinear interpolation uses the weighted sum of the surrounding four pixels: (1-dx)(1-dy)I00 + dx(1-dy)I10 + (1-dx)dy I01 + dx dy I11. The closeups show blocky steps with nearest neighbor and smoother, slightly softer edges with bilinear. Neither method reconstructs detail absent from these small archived JPEGs, and bilinear alone is not a complete antialiasing filter for strong downsampling.

A.4: Blend Images into Mosaics

Each source receives a feather weight proportional to its distance from the nearest image edge, with a small positive boundary value. Images and weights are inverse-warped into one common canvas. The output is sum(weight * color) / sum(weight), evaluated only where total weight is positive. Weights are normalized locally, so singly covered regions keep their original brightness. The comparison figures show the same registered images with hard overwrite and with feathering. Transparent pixels outside the union are preserved in the PNG results.

C(p) = sum_i [alpha_i(p) * I_i(p)] / sum_i alpha_i(p)

Implementation and Reflection

The separation between geometry, resampling, and blending makes errors easier to diagnose. A low correspondence residual does not guarantee a seamless panorama: parallax, lens distortion, moving objects, and exposure changes can violate the single-homography model or photometric assumptions. More careful fixed-center capture and locked exposure would improve these archived inputs. The selected pairs are sufficient for the displayed examples; no claim is made that the camera center was exactly fixed.

Ten deterministic numerical tests cover homography recovery with and without noise, degenerate correspondences, interpolation values, identity and translated warps, linear-ramp resampling, valid black pixels, feathered overlap, and projective-horizon rejection. All pass. The optional A.5 extension is not included; Part B follows below.

The submitted code runs with python main.py from the code/ folder. Core functions are in utils.py, with saved correspondences in config.json and inputs in data/.

B.1: Harris Corners and Adaptive Non-Maximal Suppression

The automatic pipeline starts from the same six photographs as Part A and does not read saved point coordinates. I compute grayscale luminance, Gaussian image derivatives (σ = 1), and a second-moment matrix smoothed with σ = 1.5. The Harris harmonic-mean response is det(M) / (trace(M) + ε), following Brown et al. Positive 3×3 local maxima above 0.001 times the maximum response become candidates. A 21-pixel border is excluded to fit the descriptor window. All image coordinates are (x, y).

r_i² = min_j ||p_i − p_j||², where f_i < 0.9 f_j
Keep the 500 corners with the largest suppression radii.

If no sufficiently stronger corner exists, its radius is infinite; response strength breaks ties. ANMS spreads the selected points over the image instead of concentrating them in highly textured regions. The figures show all candidate corners and the 500 selected points in the first view of each pair.

B.2: Feature Descriptor Extraction

At each selected corner, I smooth the grayscale image with a Gaussian of σ = 2 and sample an axis-aligned 8×8 grid spanning a 40×40 window. The sample centers are five pixels apart, at offsets −17.5, −12.5, …, 17.5. Bilinear sampling produces 64 values. Subtracting their mean and dividing by their standard deviation makes the descriptor invariant to positive affine brightness changes. Flat patches with standard deviation below 10⁻⁶ are rejected. This single-scale implementation does not estimate orientation or use Haar wavelets.

d = (patch − mean(patch)) / std(patch)

B.3: Feature Matching

I compute all pairwise squared Euclidean descriptor distances and find the two nearest neighbors for each source feature. A match passes if SSD₁ / SSD₂ < 0.70; this is a squared-distance ratio, equivalent to a Euclidean-distance ratio below √0.70 ≈ 0.837. The ratio rejects ambiguous patches whose best alternatives look similarly good, following the separation in Figure 6b of the paper. I additionally require mutual nearest neighbors so that reference features are not reused. Identical ties and zero second-neighbor distances are rejected. B.4: Robust Homographies and Automatic Mosaics

RANSAC is implemented from scratch. Each of 4,000 trials samples four distinct matches, uses the Part A normalized least-squares solver to estimate H, and counts matches with forward reprojection error below 3 pixels. Degenerate samples are skipped. The largest consensus wins, with total inlier error breaking ties. I refit H to all inliers and update the consensus for up to ten rounds. The random seed is fixed at 180. This budget gives 99.8% probability of sampling an all-inlier quartet at a 20% inlier fraction (1 − (1 − 0.2⁴)⁴⁰⁰⁰).

The resulting homographies feed the same inverse bilinear warping and edge-distance feathering from Part A. No SIFT, built-in feature matcher, homography estimator, RANSAC, or panorama stitcher is used in Part B. Part B: Reflection and Reproduction

ANMS makes the feature budget spatially useful; normalized broad patches tolerate illumination and small localization changes; the ratio test removes ambiguity; RANSAC rejects geometrically inconsistent matches. These stages solve different problems. More matches do not remove exposure differences, parallax, or moving objects, so the final panorama can retain seams even with a small fitted error.

Six additional deterministic tests check ANMS selection, descriptor normalization and gain/bias invariance, ambiguous-match rejection, four-point RANSAC recovery with 40% outliers, degenerate rejection, and Harris detection on a known square. Together with the ten Part A tests, all 16 pass. All three automatic mosaics are regenerated directly from the supplied JPEGs. No additional photos were needed for these results; the original fixed-camera-center capture condition remains unverified.

Run python main.py in code/ to reproduce Parts A and B, or add --part b for only automatic stitching. Dependencies are NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. The submission includes core geometry in utils.py, automatic matching in features.py, and one runner. Figure-building and packaging scripts remain outside the submitted code. Optional B.5 extensions are not included (extra credit for CS180, required for CS280A).
